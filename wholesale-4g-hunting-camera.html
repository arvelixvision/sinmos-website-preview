Wholesale 4G Hunting Cameras: LTE Bands, SIM Support and OEM Explained

A 4G hunting camera is the premium end of the trail camera market — and the one importers most often get wrong. Here is the wholesale checklist, starting with the detail that decides whether your customer ever gets a signal.

Searching for a wholesale 4G hunting camera usually means you are targeting a premium, real-time segment — buyers who want photos delivered to their phone from remote land without driving out to the camera. It is the highest-margin corner of the trail camera market, and also the one with the most avoidable failures. After 10 years shipping cellular cameras from Shenzhen, here is what actually matters when you buy them in bulk.

1. LTE Bands — The #1 Thing Importers Get Wrong

A 4G camera is only as good as the frequency bands its radio supports. The EU and the US use different LTE bands, and a unit configured for one market may sit in the forest with a full battery and no signal in the other.

This is not a small detail — it is the single most common reason a wholesale 4G shipment underperforms. Our cellular models are therefore shipped in two explicit versions:

MarketSupported LTE Bands
EU versionB1 / B3 / B7 / B8 / B20
US versionB2 / B4 / B5 / B12 / B13 / B66 / B71

Rule of thumb: Order the band version for the market you are selling into. If you sell to both, split the stock — do not assume one "world" version covers everything.

2. BYO-SIM — The Cost Lever Your Customer Cares About

The recurring cost of a cellular camera is the data plan. Brand-tied plans typically run $5–20 per camera per month, and that number is the first thing a serious buyer asks about. Cameras that support BYO-SIM (bring your own SIM) accept a standard prepaid data SIM from any local carrier, which is how a buyer keeps the running cost under their own control.

When you buy wholesale, confirm BYO-SIM support explicitly. A camera that only works on a proprietary plan is a harder sell and a stickier cost for your end customer.

4. Battery, Waterproofing & Certifications

  • Battery: non-solar models use 8× AA (up to 6 months); solar models add a 5200–7800mAh built-in pack
  • Waterproof: IP65 across the 4G line — verified, not just claimed
  • Certification: CE / FCC / RoHS, and you should request the certificates named to the exact model and band version

Because a 4G camera contains a cellular radio, certification is not optional — it is a legal requirement in most markets. Insist on the model-specific paper before the bulk order, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to check LTE bands when buying a 4G trail camera?
Yes. The EU and US use different LTE bands, so a camera configured for one market may not find a reliable signal in the other. Ours ship as an EU version (B1/B3/B7/B8/B20) and a US version (B2/B4/B5/B12/B13/B66/B71) — order the version for the market you sell into.

Can a 4G trail camera use my own SIM card?
If it supports BYO-SIM, yes — a standard prepaid data SIM from any local carrier works, which usually costs far less than a brand plan of $5–20 per camera per month.

What resolution should a wholesale 4G hunting camera support?
60MP photos with 4K or higher video is the current benchmark. Our 4G models range from 60MP/4K (PR802LTE) to 60MP/8K (PR801LTE), all with 0.3s trigger and IP65.
