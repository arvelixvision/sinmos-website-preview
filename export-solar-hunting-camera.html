Exporting Solar Hunting Cameras: The Specs, Certs and MOQ Buyers Must Check

"Solar" is the most requested feature on a hunting camera — and the one most often oversold. Here is how to verify a real solar setup, the documents you need to export it, and how to structure a wholesale order.

If you are looking to export a solar hunting camera, you are usually selling to a buyer who wants "set it and forget it" — a camera that runs through a whole season without anyone driving out to swap batteries. That promise is exactly what solar delivers, and exactly what a cheap copy fakes. After 10 years building trail cameras in Shenzhen, here is the checklist before you commit to a container.

1. Solar — Verify the Panel + Battery Pair, Not the Label

A genuine solar camera has two parts working together: a solar panel that recharges a built-in battery. What to check:

  • Built-in battery capacity — the buffer that keeps the camera running on dark days
  • A solar panel that is actually included, not a mount for one the buyer must source
  • A fallback (8× AA) for heavy cloud cover or deep winter

Our solar line uses two built-in capacities — 5200mAh and 7800mAh — each paired with a solar panel. PR903G, PR903DW and H1W list AA backup; H2G uses its built-in 7800mAh battery and solar panel, with no AA batteries. Runtime depends on battery capacity, sunlight and usage; H2G has no AA backup.

2. Solar + What? Match Connectivity to the Site

Solar handles power; it does not decide how the photos reach the buyer. Our solar models split into two connectivity groups:

How to position it: Solar + 4G (PR903G, H2G) is for remote land with cellular coverage and no nearby power or router. Solar + WiFi (PR903DW, H1W) is for farms, lodges and properties where nearby app access is practical.

3. Night Vision & Trigger

Solar does not change the optics. A solar hunting camera is only as good as its low-light performance. Ours use 850nm infrared (up to 20m) on the solar line with a 0.3s trigger and 120° detection — enough to fire on moving game and shoot cleanly in the dark, when most hunting photos are taken.

4. Certifications & Battery Shipping — The Export Part Most Buyers Skip

Because a solar camera contains a built-in lithium battery, exporting it involves two separate document sets. Get both before you book the shipment:

  • For the market you sell into: model-specific CE / FCC / RoHS certificates, named to the exact model number
  • For freight: the battery's UN38.3 test and a safety data sheet (SDS / MSDS) — carriers will not move lithium-battery cargo without them

This is the step that surprises new importers. A camera can be perfect on the bench and still get stuck at the airport without the battery paperwork. Ask for both sets up front.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does solar power add to a hunting camera?
A solar panel keeps the built-in battery topped up so the camera can run through a full season without battery swaps — the main reason to buy a solar model for remote sites.

What battery capacities do the solar models use?
Two built-in options — 5200mAh (PR903G, PR903DW) and 7800mAh (H2G, H1W), each with a solar panel. AA backup is listed for PR903G, PR903DW and H1W; H2G uses its built-in 7800mAh battery and solar panel, with no AA batteries.

Do solar cameras connect over 4G or WiFi?
Both groups exist. PR903G and H2G are solar + 4G LTE; PR903DW and H1W are solar + WiFi. Pick based on whether the site has cellular coverage or nearby WiFi app access.

What documents do I need to export a solar camera?
Model-specific CE / FCC / RoHS for your market, plus the battery's UN38.3 test and SDS/MSDS for freight. Request all of them before booking the shipment.
