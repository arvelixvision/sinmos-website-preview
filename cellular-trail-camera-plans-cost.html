Cellular Trail Cameras: The Real Cost of Monthly Plans (2026)

Brand plans run $5–20 per camera per month. Here's how to cut that by up to 70% — and what to look for before you buy.

The #1 question on r/trailcam right now: "How do people afford to have like 10 cell cams?" It's a fair question. A hunter running 10 cellular cameras on brand plans can easily pay $1,200–2,400 per year just for data. Meanwhile, the same setup with the right camera and SIM strategy costs under $400. Here's the full breakdown.

1. What Brand Plans Actually Cost

Major trail camera brands bundle their own data plans. Here's the 2026 pricing landscape from public sources and forum reports:

Brand / PlanMonthlyYearly CostNotes
Stealth Cam Standard$4–5$48–60Entry plan, limited photos
Typical brand mid-tier$10–15$120–180Unlimited-ish photos
Typical brand premium$20$240Unlimited + video

The math gets brutal at scale. Reddit users consistently report $5–20 per camera per month on brand plans — and multi-camera discounts are small ($3–5 per additional cam).

2. The BYO-SIM Hack Hunters Love

Here's the trick most hunters discover too late: many cellular trail cameras accept a standard SIM card. Instead of a brand plan, you can use a prepaid data SIM from any carrier.

Real-world examples from r/trailcam and r/Hunting:

  • Straight Talk unlimited: ~$45/month, but one plan can cover multiple cameras via sharing
  • Low-usage prepaid data SIMs: $5–10/month for 1–2GB — plenty for 500–1,500 photos
  • Annual prepaid data packs: as low as $60–100/year for a single camera

Pro tip: Before buying any cellular camera, ask two questions: "Does it support BYO-SIM?" and "Which bands does it support?" A camera locked to a brand's plan removes your only cost lever.

That's a 50–80% saving — on a 10-camera setup, it can pay for several new cameras every year.

4. Solar Changes the Whole Equation

Here's what most buyers miss: cellular cameras that run on solar transmit smarter. With a steady power supply, you can use solar-integrated cameras that only send when something happens (event-based transmission), drastically cutting data usage — sometimes to under 100MB/month.

Our PR801LTE and PR802LTE support BYO-SIM with event-based photo transmission and 60MP photos; PR801LTE records up to 8K video and PR802LTE up to 4K. Pair them with a solar model like the H2G or PR903G and your total cellular cost can drop below $5/month per camera in many regions.

5. What to Ask Before You Buy

  • Does the camera accept a standard SIM card (BYO-SIM)?
  • What network bands does it support? (Check your local carrier)
  • Is there event-based transmission to save data?
  • Can I set photo size / resolution to control data use?
  • Is there a solar version so I don't burn batteries on transmission?
  • Is the camera CE/FCC certified for legal use in your country?

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do cellular trail camera data plans cost?
Brand plans typically run $5–20 per camera per month, or $48–240 per camera per year. Multi-camera discounts are small, usually $3–5 per additional camera.

What does BYO-SIM mean?
BYO-SIM (bring your own SIM) means the camera accepts a standard SIM card, so you can use a prepaid data plan from any local carrier instead of a brand-locked plan.

How much can BYO-SIM save on a 10-camera setup?
A 10-camera brand-plan setup runs roughly $1,200–2,400 in year one, while a BYO-SIM setup on one shared unlimited plan runs about $400–700 — a saving of 50–80%.

Which cameras support BYO-SIM?
Arvelix Vision PR801LTE and PR802LTE support BYO-SIM with event-based photo transmission, and both cover EU bands (B1/B3/B7/B8/B20) and US bands (B2/B4/B5/B12/B13/B66/B71).
